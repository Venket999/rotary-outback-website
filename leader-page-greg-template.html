Greg Marlow's Rotary journey began in 1987 in Kalgoorlie, Western Australia—three decades ago. Today, as District Governor and champion of the Bed Project, he's turned a simple idea into a global initiative: transforming plastic waste into safe, durable beds for remote Australian communities.

What started with a two-year recession-driven unemployment didn't break him. Instead, it taught him resilience. That lesson became his superpower in Rotary. Over six terms as Club President in Tennant Creek, then as District Rotary Foundation Chair, and now steering an entire region, Greg has built a reputation for turning vision into impact.

"The Bed Project isn't about charity," Greg says. "It's about dignity, sustainability, and proving that Australian innovation can solve problems others ignore."

Today, that vision has already created 100+ beds across Palm Island, Mt Isa, Tennant Creek, and Kalgoorlie. But Greg isn't stopping there. He's currently raising $67,816 USD to scale to 200 beds—providing safe sleeping conditions for 200+ people while creating local employment and reducing environmental waste.

The Bed Project: Transforming Plastic Into Dignity

The Problem

Remote Australian communities often lack access to basic bedding. Damp climates and inadequate sleeping surfaces lead to health issues, particularly respiratory illness and skin conditions in vulnerable populations.

The Solution

The Bed Project manufactures durable beds from recycled plastic, complete with washable, waterproof mattress components. Each bed is designed with community input and built locally—creating jobs, skills, and pride of ownership.

Current Impact

  • ✓ 100+ beds installed across 4 regions
  • ✓ 200+ people with improved sleeping conditions
  • ✓ 15+ local jobs created through assembly & maintenance
  • ✓ 2,000+ kg of plastic diverted from landfill

Next Phase

To reach 200 beds and establish permanent maintenance programs across 8 districts, we need to raise $67,816 USD. Every $500 funds one bed + training + support.
